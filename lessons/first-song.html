Your First Song

Time to put C, G, Am, Em, and D to work. This is the same four-chord progression underneath hundreds of well-known pop and folk songs — we'll teach you the pattern, not copyrighted lyrics.

The progression: G – D – Em – C

Play each chord for four down-strums, then move to the next. Loop the whole thing over and over — that repetition is what builds real chord-switching speed.

G4 strums
D4 strums
Em4 strums
C4 strums
Why this progression? G–D–Em–C (and its rotations) is one of the most recorded chord progressions in popular music. Once it's under your fingers, you can strum along with an enormous number of real songs — search "songs with chords G D Em C" once you're ready to try it against actual recordings.

Step-by-step practice plan

  1. Day 1–2: Practice switching G → D only, slowly, with a 2-second pause between each change.
  2. Day 3–4: Add Em, then C, one at a time — same slow, deliberate switching.
  3. Day 5–7: Play the full loop at a slow, steady down-strum tempo (try 70 BPM on the strumming metronome).
  4. Week 2: Add the down-up strumming pattern from the previous lesson once switches feel automatic.
