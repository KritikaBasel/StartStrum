Introduction to Guitar

Before you touch a single string, get to know the instrument itself — the different types of guitars, and why the one in your hands works the way it does.

Three main types of guitar

Almost every guitar you'll ever see falls into one of three families. Knowing which one you have changes how it sounds, how hard the strings are to press, and what music it's suited for.

🪕 Acoustic

Steel strings, hollow wood body, no amplifier needed. Bright and loud on its own — the most common first guitar.

🎸 Electric

Thinner body, lighter steel strings, needs an amplifier to be heard properly. Easier on the fingers than acoustic.

🎻 Classical

Nylon strings, wider neck, warm mellow tone. Nylon is gentle on beginner fingertips but the wide neck takes adjustment.

Which should a beginner start with? Any of the three works. Acoustic is the most common default because it needs no extra gear, but nylon-string classical guitars are genuinely kinder on sore fingertips during your first two weeks.

Why the shape matters

A guitar is essentially a long fretted neck attached to a resonating body. Pressing a string against a fret shortens the vibrating length of that string, which raises its pitch. Every chord and note you'll ever play comes down to that one mechanic — string length and tension. Once that clicks, chord diagrams stop looking like a foreign language.

💡

Don't worry about owning the "right" guitar. The best guitar to learn on is the one you'll actually pick up every day.
