Strumming Patterns

Chords give you the notes; strumming gives you the rhythm. Start slow — timing matters more than speed.

The three basic motions

Down strum (D)

Your hand moves downward across the strings, from thickest to thinnest. This is the first motion every beginner learns.

Up strum (U)

Your hand moves upward, thinnest to thickest — lighter and quieter than a down strum. Rarely used alone; it's what fills the gaps between downs.

Down-Up (DU) — the workhorse pattern

Most beginner songs use a version of this. Try counting "1-and-2-and-3-and-4-and" while your hand keeps moving continuously, down on the number, up on the "and."

Keep your hand moving. Even on the beats you don't strum, let your hand keep swinging down-up-down-up in the air. This "constant motion" habit is what makes strumming feel steady instead of choppy.

Practice metronome

Set a tempo and strum along. Starting at 60–80 BPM and building up gradually beats jumping straight to full speed.
